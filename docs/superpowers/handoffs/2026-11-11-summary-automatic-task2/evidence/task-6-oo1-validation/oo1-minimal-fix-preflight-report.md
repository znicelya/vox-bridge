# OO1 minimum original-source repair preflight

**Conclusion.** The smallest scheduler repair is an owned blocking boundary around the existing claim, with at most two actual in-flight operations, genuine handles retained by the original generation, and the original two async workers awaiting their results. A worker-local spawn_blocking handle alone is insufficient protection against worker loss: the controller currently ignores worker join errors, and an empty or not-yet-acquired claim has no reap frame. Keep claim capacity and completion tied to actual operation termination and join, independently of shutdown observers.

**Claims alone do not establish covering OO1 GREEN.** The specific ordinary-stop and App-close paths still contain synchronous SQLite acquisitions in scheduler shutdown, session lifecycle persistence, direction persistence, and the original summary delivery worker. These can occupy both executor threads after claims are moved. The genuine run-002 fixture can support a fresh RED/GREEN validation of a repair, but its single “any heartbeat increase” predicate should be strengthened to detect starvation later in the hold. No implementation or post-offload measurement was performed for this preflight.

**Scope.** This follows [the minimum-repair brief](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/.superpowers/sdd/2026-11-09-session-summary-continuation/task-6-oo1-validation/oo1-minimal-fix-preflight-brief.md:1). ## Original ownership and the minimum claim boundary

The [generation controller](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs.rs:248) holds the original strong OperationLease, creates a token channel of capacity two, and spawns exactly two workers. Each [worker](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs.rs:622) receives one token and calls synchronous claim at line 646. No second claim is dispatched by that worker while its current claim or handler is pending.

The [existing claim](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs.rs:760) acquires the real SQLite transaction before checking control.stopping, closed, or an empty queue. SQLite acquires the synchronous connection mutex at [sqlite.rs:153](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/storage/sqlite.rs:153). Inside the acquired transaction, the lock order remains SQLite → generation control → reap registry → frame. A selected queued row is decoded and identified, registered before Running, added to the active cancellation map, and committed. PendingClaim becomes a committed Frame only after the real transaction succeeds; rollback removes the pending registration and active cancellation entry.

The proposed minimum changes are:

1. At start_owned, create two private, generation-owned retained handle slots, one for each original worker. At the existing worker claim call, clone Arc store/control/closed/reap into a blocking closure, retain a strong clone of the original operation lease for its actual lifetime, and execute the unchanged synchronous claim inside that closure. The transaction and its borrowed SQLite values stay entirely inside the blocking operation.
2. The original worker awaits the genuine result through the retained handle. It must not select shutdown/caller cancellation against that handle and discard the result. A committed claim still belongs to the original worker/frame/handler lifecycle; an observer error or SQL status replacement does not discharge it.
3. Retain the handle in generation-owned storage until actual join, including when the worker future is lost. Ordinary shutdown observers already observe a watch rather than owning these workers, so a plain awaited spawn_blocking would yield correctly on that normal path. The retained slots are needed because dropping a worker-local blocking handle detaches actual work, while the controller's line-317 join loop currently ignores JoinError. Drain leftover handles and record abnormal worker/claim joins before any generation completion. An unconsumed committed frame must fail closed under the existing unfinished-factory rule, rather than being marked as a completed handler.
4. Bound actual submitted/entered-but-unjoined claims to two, including blocking-pool-queued closures. Acquire any admission permit before submission and keep it until actual completion; do not release it on timeout or observer drop, retry into a growing queue, or use the global blocking pool's limit as the scheduler capacity. The two fixed workers already bound normal dispatch; a separate claim drain/handle owner makes that bound and shutdown proof survive worker loss.
5. Keep process ownership in the live generation and issued operation, with weak factories after completion. Remove joined handles/results so a retained factory or context cannot retain the process after generation completion.

Do not fund these claims from the existing [sixteen child-operation slots](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs/child_operations.rs:8). Those slots are shared by real handler children, retain each actual process lease through the last clone, and drain only after workers/cleanup. The [reap frame cap remains eighteen](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs/reap.rs:9); the claim boundary must not create fictitious job frames, increase this cap, or replace the raw rowid/payload identity witness. The token channel stays two, unfinished-job bound stays thirty-two, and parser gate stays one.

The [reap registry](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs/reap.rs:128) only registers an actual selected row after acquisition. Thus neither an empty queue nor zero frames proves that an outstanding blocking acquisition joined. Preserve the controller's final sequence: stop admission/cancel active work, stop token production, join original workers and retained claim/maintenance operations, complete owned handler cleanup, drain sixteen actual children, quiesce frame completion publication, release the generation lease, mark this generation Joined, then publish completion. Failed joins or unfinished factories remain a closed, unsuccessful generation.

## Scheduler shutdown must also yield

[signal_shutdown](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs.rs:493) already closes atomic/watch admission without SQL or a generation mutex. Preserve that immediate signal.

[request_shutdown](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs.rs:497) then holds the scheduler state mutex, sets control.stopping and active cancellation, releases control, and synchronously acquires SQLite at line 513 to cancel up to thirty-two queued jobs through the original terminal hook. It still acquires SQLite when the queue is empty. Post-transaction get_job reads at line 535 are synchronous too. [shutdown](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs.rs:543) invokes this work before starting its async completion timeout. On timeout/completion error it holds state and calls synchronous interrupt at line 573; [interrupt](/mnt/data/workspack/github/vox-bridge/.worktrees/vox-bridge-implementation/src-tauri/src/intelligence/jobs.rs:851) takes another real transaction. Drop calls request_shutdown at line 607.

For a coherent scheduler repair, split the immediate in-memory request from those SQL operations. Under a short state lock, capture the original generation UUID and narrow store/handler/control/event ports, then release the state/control guards before any blocking submission or await. Give each closing generation one retained, coalesced maintenance operation at a time for queued cancellation, read/publication, and requested interruption. Keep its handle/lease through actual SQL return and join; repeated shutdown observers must share that owner rather than submit another blocked closure.

Queued cancellation and timeout interruption must be able to run while original workers drain. Postponing all maintenance until worker join would leave queued jobs untouched behind a stubborn handler. Include maintenance completion in generation retirement, retain the fresh UUID check when an observer resumes, and prevent an old deferred interruption from modifying a rearmed generation. Do not move a state/control mutex guard into the closure while it waits on SQLite. The same-key App/audio/delivery close fences continue to govern their original owners.

**Timeout contract to preserve explicitly.** The existing free-store test at jobs/tests.rs:217 expects Interrupted already durable when its short shutdown returns an error, while real handler/native ownership remains retained. If an observation timeout is allowed to return while its SQL maintenance is still blocked, it must report pending/error without asserting durable interruption or releasing ownership; later real SQL completion must remain owned. The author's design and tests must reconcile these observations. Merely offloading request_shutdown and then forgetting its handle, or synchronously running interrupt after the observer timeout, leaves the relevant defect.

## Limited additional seams for a covering repair

The source-based covering candidate is to make the named SQL calls yield inside their existing actual owners, rather than replace LocalStore or add another executor:

- Keep session phase and terminal/error persistence serial in the original autonomous observer, with at most one submitted-but-unjoined storage operation for that session. Retain the actual session scope until those operations and the original runtime join finish. Recheck the original stop/publication fence after phase persistence; preserve terminal lifecycle and journal in the same original UPDATE, and notification after durable commit, bus close and active-operation release.
- Keep each direction's diagnostic/final transcript persistence serial and bounded by its original actor. Up to two original direction tasks exist; avoid accumulating one queued task per frame or diagnostic. Native capture suppression/stop and actual native joins remain the original producers. Persist a final before consuming transcript identity/sequence or publishing it. Diagnostic failure handling and forced cleanup accounting must still execute.
- Make the original delivery worker's page query yield, including empty scans. Its existing child factory already has one actual slot and upgrades the original weak process lease at terminal.rs:417. A short page/read operation can use that owned scope, retaining its real handle; release/join it before one enters the next child, so the one-slot capacity cannot self-deadlock. Preserve thirty-two-row pages, 200 ms wake behavior, same-key seal/receiver-EOF order and genuine worker/child/closer joins. If one reaches a terminal journal, the synchronous attempt read (:588), error verification (:637) and configuration-fallback read (:565) must also yield; leaving them synchronous would only move the stall.

This identifies the narrow owners/call sites to address, not a whole-store migration. Offloading one member of a possible pair is not a source proof that all the other parallel callers leave executor capacity available. Root owns the final minimum-scope decision.

**Remaining scope risk.** Nonempty jobs add synchronous notice, terminal transaction and post-terminal get_job in worker at jobs.rs:680/:701/:732. They do not run in the empty-queue counterexample. They can reuse the original worker's bounded storage ownership without extra workers if included in the repair; otherwise a GREEN empty-queue fixture cannot establish availability during actual handler completion. Startup/recovery, larger/default production executor pools and scheduling frequency were not measured here. Native capacity, release, same-key rearm and paid/physical acceptance keep their prior dispositions.

## Can the genuine OO1 fixture supply covering RED/GREEN?

**Yes for this controlled counterexample, with a fresh authorized fixture/version and additional ownership assertions; no for a claims-only or unchanged weak-predicate proof of complete availability.** Preserve real AppState/LocalStore, the two original workers and two-worker Tokio executor, actual ordinary stop/App close, the independent monotonic OS holder/release, and the original retained native parent. Keep both actual token-owning workers established before acquisition, non-waiting probes immediately before and inside the real transaction, and actual post-release closure/owner joins. Blocking-pool entry thread IDs may differ from original executor IDs; validate two distinct actual acquisition threads and their original-worker provenance rather than require literal ThreadId(3)/(4).

Measure first action poll, actual capture closure, primed timer completion and successive heartbeat progress as separate events. Require progress across separated early/middle/late hold windows, or a declared maximum heartbeat gap with a scheduling allowance, rather than merely one increase before release. This must detect an early successful tick/100 ms timer followed by lifecycle/diagnostic/delivery starvation. Record the monotonic timestamps so a later stall is visible. Durable terminal SQL completion may correctly remain pending while SQLite is held; it is not the availability assertion. Close success/reopen still requires the original held native parent's release and the genuine same-key joins.

Run-002 remains the accepted dual-claim RED at tree d1dd2c7aa27501ac817c24f3ada28778dfd1cb86. Run-001 remains insufficient entry evidence. Future original-source RED/GREEN should use newly authorized artifacts, retain the production SQLite and close producers, check the strengthened fixture fails for its availability assertion before repair, then validate the repaired actual seams. Do not rewrite either historical run, its probes/snapshots, or the metadata-timing correction. Focused blocked-claim/shutdown tests complement the end-to-end fixture; the empty fixture does not cover nonempty worker terminal SQL or all production pool configurations.
